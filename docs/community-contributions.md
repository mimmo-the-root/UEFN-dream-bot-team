# Sharing lessons with the community

The genre skills get better when creators share what their maps taught them. Sharing is **voluntary**, **off by default**, and limited to lessons.

## What is shared, and what never is

Shared, one lesson at a time and only after you press **Contribute**: genre, map type, section, and the three lines of the lesson (statement, condition, action), with your GitHub handle on the pull request.

Never shared: map names, Verse code, devices, blueprints, island codes, notes, evidence, task ids. Those stay on your computer. The kit sends nothing by itself.

## Only lessons from a published map

A lesson can be shared only if it was learned from a map that is **published**. The kit checks it: the project has an island code and Epic has public data for it. A lesson from a map that is not published stays on your computer, and the Contribute button explains why. When the map is published the lessons become shareable.

## How to contribute

1. Skills page, section **Community**: switch on "Contribute to the community".
2. Press **Contribute** next to one of your approved lessons. You see the exact JSON that would leave your computer.
3. **Open GitHub**: the new-file page opens with the lesson filled in. Rename the file to your GitHub handle (for example `ada.json`) and press *Propose new file*. GitHub opens a pull request from your own account.

By proposing the file you agree that the anonymized lesson becomes part of the kit under the repository licence, and that your handle is credited.

## What happens next

An Action checks the pull request (only your lesson file, valid format, screening against anything that tries to instruct an assistant). Nothing is deleted: a lesson moves through the levels *screened, hypothesis, confirmed, archived*. A single lesson is published as a **hypothesis** ("to verify against the map's code"); with a second creator, or the maintainer's approval, it becomes **confirmed**. A curator proposes the promotion and the maintainer validates it before any release.

Creators receive the community lessons as proposals they approve on their Skills page, like their own.

## Reputation

Your handle is shown on your lessons. The score grows when your lessons are confirmed (+3, and +1 for each other creator who sent the same lesson) and drops when one is revoked (-5). New handles start at zero and get the stricter checks.

## Reporting a wrong lesson

Use *Submit an issue* on the What's new page (or the "Wrong community lesson" form on GitHub). A lesson that is wrong or unsafe is revoked and leaves everyone's skills at the next update.
